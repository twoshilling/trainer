Six checks before you speak

These are the slips that come up most. Every conversation here sets them for you on purpose.

  1. Who is doing it? One man: maiñ kardaa aañ. We or they: karde aañ. A woman: kardii.
  2. Where is nai? Directly in front of the verb: hor nai chaahidii, hale nai khaadhii.
  3. Does it come to you? Hunger, wanting, knowing and feelings start with mainuñ. Things you do start with maiñ.
  4. Who are you talking to? Elders get tusiiñ, and the verb ends in -o. Cousins get tuuñ and a bare verb.
  5. Where is the little word? After the noun, never before: London bich, kamm te, mere toñ. And meraa bends to mere in front of it.
  6. Is it the past? "Ate" and "did" match the thing, and the doer takes ne: Jassi ne samose khaadhe. "Went" and "came" match the person, with no ne.

How to read the spelling

aa ii uu
Doubled vowels are held long: chaa, jii, tuuñ.
ñ
The vowel before it goes through the nose: maiñ, haañ.
ṭ ḍ ṇ ṛ ḷ
A dot underneath: curl the tongue tip back to the roof of the mouth.
th kh ph
A puff of air after the consonant. Never the English "th".
à ù ì
The voice starts low on that vowel, then lifts: kàr (home), pùkkh (hunger), kàṭṭ (less), chùuṭh (a lie).
-daa / -dii
Your lines use a man's endings. A woman swaps -daa for -dii and gayaa for gaii.
